Solar Activity

.24 hr Summary...
Solar activity was at low levels in the past 24h. There are 5 active
regions on the visible solar disk. The largest Region 4579 (N16W09,
Hsx/alpha) was responsible for the largest flare of the period: a C9.6
flare that peaked at 20/1130 UTC. The most magnetically complex Region
4581 (S07E25, Dao/beta) continued quiet, as its intermediary spots
decayed and reorganized. The remaining regions showed some decay as they
rotated towards the west limb.

A filament that erupted near Region 4579 in the previous reported
period, around 19/0730 UTC, was associated with a faint CME (poorly
detected by the available coronagraphs). Analysis suggest a glancing
blow near-Earth on Nov 23, that might create a combined effect with the
anticipated arrival of a CME from 14 Nov. No other Earth-directed CMEs
were identified in the available coronagraph imagery during the period.

.Forecast...
Solar activity is likely to be at low levels, with a slight chance for
M-class flares (R1-R2, Minor-Moderate) on 21-23 Nov.

Energetic Particle

.24 hr Summary...
The greater than 2 MeV electron flux remained at moderate levels. The
greater than 10 MeV proton flux continued at background levels.

.Forecast...
The greater than 2 MeV electron flux is expected to be at moderate
levels on 21-22 Nov, reaching high levels on 23 Nov. The greater than 10
MeV proton flux is expected to continue at background levels on through
23 Nov.

Solar Wind

.24 hr Summary...
Solar wind parameters reflected undisturbed conditions near Earth in the
past 24h. Total interplanetary magnetic field (Bt) slightly oscillated
around 5 nT, while its north-south (Bz) component remained mostly
neutral (+/- 5 nT) during the period. Solar wind speeds continued
gradually decreasing, from 400 km/s down to 350 km/s levels. Phi was
positive through the whole reported period.

.Forecast...
Continued background solar wind conditions are likely on 21-22 Nov.
Slight enhancements are possible on 23 Nov due to a low confidence
glancing blow of CMEs from 14 Nov and 19 Nov.
