Solar Activity

.24 hr Summary...
Solar activity reached moderate levels with two M-flares from Region
4578 (S11W73, Dri/beta-gamma): an M1.1 at 17/1549 UTC and an M1.7 at
17/2139 UTC. Two new regions were numbered this period. The first was
Region 4580 (N15W89, Cro/beta), which while it continued to grow was
quiet. A new region in the SE emerged rapidly midway through the
reporting period and began producing low-level C-flares; this region was
numbered as Region 4581 (S09E67, Cro/gamma). Region 4578 exhibited rapid
flux emergence later in the reporting period, and Region 4581 continued
to evolve throughout the period. Region 4575 (S09W15, Hrx/alpha)
definitively lost its trailing spots and became a H-type. The remaining
regions were either stable or in slight decay.

A CME associated with the M1.1 first became visible in LASCO C2 imagery
at approximately 17/1600 UTC. Modeling indicates that this eruption
becomes tangled with the CMEs from 16 Nov and that the structure may
produce a glancing blow early on 22 Nov. An eruption near Region 4579
was observed by SUVI around 17/1930 UTC and an associated CME trajectory
was modeled. No Earth-directed component was identified at that event
and no other Earth-directed CME was observed at available coronagraph
imagery during the period.

.Forecast...
Solar activity is expected to be low with a slight chance for M-class
flares (R1-R2, Minor-Moderate) over 18-20 Nov.

Energetic Particle

.24 hr Summary...
The greater than 2 MeV electron flux reached high levels with a peak
flux of 3,668 pfu observed at 17/1710 UTC. The greater than 10 MeV
proton flux was at background levels.

.Forecast...
The greater than 2 MeV electron flux is expected to reach moderate
levels on 18 Nov, with an anticipated return to high levels on 19-20
Nov. The greater than 10 MeV proton flux is expected to continue at
background levels on through 20 Nov.

Solar Wind

.24 hr Summary...
The solar wind environment reflected a solar sector boundary crossing
(SSBC) at approximately 17/0730 UTC which gradually lead to slightly
elevated parameters. Total magnetic field (Bt) was mostly ambient-like
until the SSBC, after which it initially jumped to 7 nT and began to
gradually increase, ending the reporting period around 9 nT, with a peak
of 10 nT at 17/2059 UTC. The Bz (north-south) component also deflected
southward at this time, averaging approximately -4 nT until
approximately 17/1900 UTC, where it became extremely variable, with
several maximum southward deflections of -8 nT. However, solar wind
speeds remained relatively low throughout the reporting period, never
reaching above 360 km/s. Phi became positive at approximately 17/0732
UTC (SSBC).

.Forecast...
Weak enhancements in solar wind parameters are likely to continue
through 18 Nov as a positive polarity CH HSS continues to move into a
geoeffective position, coupled with the possible arrival of the 14 Nov
CME. These enhancements are anticipated to begin waning through 19 Nov,
with a solar sector boundary crossing anticipated on 20 Nov.

Geospace

.24 hr Summary...
The geomagnetic field was at quiet to unsettled levels during the
period.

.Forecast...
Active to isolated G1 (Minor) levels are likely on 18 Nov as the CH HSS
continues to move into a geoeffective position along with the possible
arrival of the 14 Nov CME late in the period. Largely unsettled
conditions are anticipated on 19-20 Nov, with a chance for isolated
active periods on 20 Nov due to an anticipated solar sector boundary
crossing.
